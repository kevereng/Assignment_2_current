## What are the advantages of open source software?

For me, a major advantage of open-source software revolves around the question of ownership. Whether it’s media, research software, or cloud services, many of the online products that we currently pay for follow a subscription model where access is granted to us in exchange for a monthly or annual fee. This not only means that we pay more in the long run, but we can also have our access changed or revoked at any time if the company chooses to limit access (ex. by increasing the price beyond what we can afford), if they change what they offer (ex, Netflix changing their programming) or if they change their model completely (ex, not strictly subscription-based, but I can no longer access movies that I bought on iTunes 15 years ago).
</p>
<p>
Open-source, on the other hand, gives you unlimited, and forever access to programs, as well as the ability to contribute, adapting them to your needs as they develop. Not only does access to the code remain public, but if anyone wants to make changes, they have the freedom to do so, with communities organised around specific software having the ability to essentially decide democratically what features they want to see implemented (Engard, 2010).
</p>
<p>
A personal example of an open-source software that I would benefit from is Gimp. As a photographer, I currently use a subscription to Photoshop to process my photos. However, since I remain at the mercy of Adobe, switching to Gimp would not only prove to be a cheaper option, but I could have a say in what sort of changes I would want to see over time, and potentially even add them myself (if I became good enough at coding).
</p>
<p>
From a university perspective, using open-source software is often likely the best option, as it not only gives them the most amount of control over software features, but also allows students researchers to store research data on their own servers. While this obviously requires a lot of resources, it keeps sensitive information out of the hands of larger corporations, which may lead to security breaches out of the university’s control. 
</p>
