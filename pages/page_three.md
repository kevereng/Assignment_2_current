## Which open source project would be useful in your major, and why?

<p>
As an Intercultural Studies student, much of the research that we do involves collecting qualitative research data through interviews, in order to understand how different meanings are made across different cultures. In order to make this data easier to comb through and cite, transcription software has become a fundamental part of this sort of research.
</p>
<p>
NoScribe was developed by Kai Dröge, a German sociologist, in order to transcribe, review, and edit interviews locally (noScribe, n.d.a.). This adds an important layer of security, as running the program locally removes the risk of an external server being hacked, meaning that it complies with GDPR (at least as far as the creator thinks) (noScribe, n.d.b).
</p>
<p>

### Week 41 checks:

1. NoScribe is licensed under the GNU General Public License, meaning that it is free “to run [...] to study and change [...] to redistribute [...] and to distribute modified versions” (GitHub, 2026) (GNU, 2025).
2. The last change was committed on November 9, 2026.
3. It has twenty contributors.
4. It seems to be run by volunteers, but users have the option to contribute through a Ko-fi link on the program’s website.

</p>
<p>
I could easily see myself using this software to transcribe and annotate interviews, as this is something I will likely have to do quite often for my master's thesis. I also would not feel trapped by using it, as there is no subscription fee or organisational pressure to use it.
</p>
